# Recorded demo video

Created September 27, 2026. The local export is `Same-Moon-Demo.mp4` in the sibling `same-moon-video` Downloads folder. It is 1920 × 1080, approximately 2 minutes 31 seconds, H.264 with AAC narration, about 6.5 MB. A separate `same-moon-demo.srt` subtitle file and the editable narration script are in the same folder.

## What is shown

Actual captures of the working GitHub Pages web demo: family setup, viewing windows, timeline, animated map, multilingual message preview, and the example postcard. The illustrated postcard is labeled as an example. A separate verification card describes the 24 automated tests and pending live checks. No real iMessage conversation, paid Claude request, or real family Moon session was filmed or claimed.

The voiceover is computer-generated with the installed macOS Samantha voice, using no paid API. Browser footage was sampled at about 10 frames per second, edited into a 30 fps video with holds where needed for narration, and given burned-in captions. Native macOS screen recording stalled and the computer-use tool did not permit Terminal app control; the finished video uses the recorded web demo rather than terminal footage.

The MP4 decoded without errors. Its resolution, duration, audio track, and representative frames were checked. With the user's approval, it was uploaded to YouTube as **Unlisted**: https://youtu.be/C0xMxBDkJ1E. The link was saved in the Devpost video field, and the embedded video was verified on the project preview. The project remains a draft; final Submit was not pressed.

## Narration and edit

### 0:00 — Different time zones. Same Moon.

Different time zones. Same Moon. Families living far apart often say they're looking at the same moon. But that is only possible when it's above the horizon for everyone. Same Moon finds that shared moment, and turns it into a small family ritual.

### 0:16 — 01 / Tell us where everyone is

Start with a sentence you would actually send in a group chat. I'm at Wash U. Mom is in Shanghai and prefers Chinese. Jia is in Toronto. The demo creates a family roster with each person's city and preferred language.

### 0:31 — 02 / Find a time that works for everyone

Here is the next shared window for this example family. Six in the morning in Saint Louis, seven in the evening in Shanghai, and seven in the morning in Toronto. The overlap lasts forty five minutes. Everyone sees their own local time.

### 0:46 — 03 / See the overlap

The timeline explains why timing matters. Pale bars show when the Moon is up for each person. Gold marks the overlap while everyone is awake. The dotted sections reveal the tradeoff: the Moon is visible, but somebody would have to wake up early.

### 1:02 — 04 / Play the next 72 hours

Press play, and the next seventy two hours unfold on the world map. Watch the moonlit region move, and the family pins change as the Moon rises and sets. These positions come from the astronomy engine running in the browser. The animation makes the calculation visible.

### 1:20 — 05 / Know where to look

At the chosen moment, each person gets a useful viewing instruction in their own language. Ethan looks west as the Moon sets. Mom looks east as it rises. The height is described in fists above the horizon. This web preview shows the messages designed for the family chat.

### 1:37 — 06 / Keep the shared moment

Afterwards, the two photos become one postcard, with names, local times, and the distance between them. The skies shown here are the demo's illustrations, ready to be replaced with uploaded photos. Eleven thousand six hundred kilometers apart. One Moon.

### 1:56 — Built, tested, and clearly scoped

The project includes a Photon Spectrum chat adapter, a scheduler, seven language templates, and postcard generation. All twenty four automated tests pass, including the terminal chat flow and iPhone photo conversion. Real iMessage delivery and live Claude calls are still pending. The viewing instructions come from deterministic engine templates.

### 2:19 — A shared moment, across the world

Same Moon is built around a simple idea: a photo can show what someone saw, but a shared moment lets you look up together. Different time zones. Same Moon.
